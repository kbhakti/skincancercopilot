# Benign Keratosis-like Lesions (BKL)

## Overview
This category groups several common, non-cancerous keratotic skin
growths that can look similar on imaging, most notably seborrheic
keratosis, solar lentigo (age spot / large plaque-like keratosis), and
lichen-planus-like keratosis. They are extremely common, especially with
increasing age, and are not caused by malignant transformation.

## Typical Appearance
- Seborrheic keratoses: waxy, "stuck-on" appearing papules or plaques,
  tan to dark brown or black, often with a warty, crumbly surface
  texture. Can appear anywhere except palms, soles, and mucous
  membranes.
- Solar lentigines: flat, uniformly tan-to-brown patches on
  sun-exposed skin (face, hands, forearms), sometimes called "age
  spots" or "liver spots."
- Lichen-planus-like keratosis: a solitary, inflamed, pink-brown patch,
  sometimes an evolving/regressing solar lentigo or seborrheic
  keratosis.

## Risk Factors
- Increasing age (seborrheic keratoses become very common after age 50).
- Cumulative sun exposure (for solar lentigines).
- Possible genetic predisposition (seborrheic keratoses can run in
  families).

## Diagnosis
Usually diagnosed clinically/dermoscopically by their characteristic
"stuck-on" appearance and comedo-like openings. Biopsy is reserved for
lesions with atypical features that cannot be confidently distinguished
from melanoma or pigmented BCC.

## Treatment
Treatment is optional and cosmetic in most cases:
- **Cryotherapy**, **curettage**, or **shave removal** if the lesion is
  irritated, itchy, catches on clothing, or for cosmetic reasons.
- No treatment is medically necessary if the lesion is stable and
  clearly benign.

## Prognosis & Follow-up
Excellent — these are benign lesions with no malignant potential.
Routine skin checks are still worthwhile because a changing or atypical
lesion should always be re-evaluated to rule out melanoma or pigmented
BCC mimicking a keratosis.

## When to Seek Care
Evaluation is warranted if a lesion changes rapidly, bleeds
spontaneously, becomes painful, or does not fit the classic "stuck-on"
appearance, since these can be signs it is not a simple benign
keratosis.
