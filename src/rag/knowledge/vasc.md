# Vascular Lesions (VASC)

## Overview
This category covers benign lesions formed by blood vessels in or under
the skin, including cherry angiomas, angiokeratomas, pyogenic
granulomas, and vascular malformations. They are generally common and
benign, though a rapidly growing or bleeding vascular lesion (like a
pyogenic granuloma) can be alarming in appearance and sometimes needs
treatment for bleeding control.

## Typical Appearance
- **Cherry angiomas:** small, smooth, bright red-to-purple round
  papules, very common with increasing age, usually on the trunk.
- **Angiokeratomas:** dark red-to-black, slightly warty papules caused
  by dilated superficial blood vessels with overlying thickened skin.
- **Pyogenic granuloma:** a rapidly growing, friable, red nodule that
  bleeds easily, often after minor trauma; common in pregnancy and in
  children.
- **Vascular malformations:** flat or raised, pink-to-purple patches
  present from birth or early life (e.g., port-wine stains), stable in
  proportion to body growth.

## Risk Factors
- Increasing age (cherry angiomas).
- Pregnancy and certain medications (pyogenic granuloma).
- Minor trauma at the site (pyogenic granuloma).
- Vascular malformations are typically congenital/developmental rather
  than risk-factor driven.

## Diagnosis
Usually diagnosed clinically and dermoscopically based on the
characteristic red-to-purple vascular color and structure (e.g.,
"red-blue lacunae"). Biopsy may be used for atypical or rapidly
changing lesions to exclude amelanotic melanoma or vascular
malignancy, which can rarely mimic benign vascular lesions.

## Treatment
- Most vascular lesions do not require treatment.
- **Pyogenic granulomas** are often removed (shave excision with
  cautery, or curettage) due to a tendency to bleed.
- **Cherry angiomas / angiokeratomas** can be treated with laser or
  electrocautery for cosmetic reasons if desired.
- **Vascular malformations** (e.g., port-wine stains) are often treated
  with pulsed-dye laser for cosmetic improvement.

## Prognosis & Follow-up
Excellent — these lesions are benign. Follow-up is mainly warranted if
a lesion is rapidly growing, ulcerating, or otherwise behaving
atypically, to exclude a more concerning diagnosis.

## When to Seek Care
A vascular-appearing lesion that grows quickly, bleeds repeatedly, or
does not fit the classic benign patterns described above should be
evaluated by a dermatologist.
