# Dermatofibroma (DF)

## Overview
Dermatofibroma is a common, benign fibrous nodule of the skin, thought
to arise from a reactive proliferation of fibroblasts/histiocytes,
sometimes following a minor injury such as an insect bite or a small
cut. It is entirely benign and does not require treatment unless
symptomatic or diagnostically uncertain.

## Typical Appearance
- A firm, small (usually less than 1 cm) nodule, most common on the
  lower legs, though it can occur anywhere.
- Skin-colored to red-brown or dark brown, often slightly raised.
- Classic finding: the "dimple sign" — the lesion dimples inward when
  pinched from the sides, which helps distinguish it clinically from
  other pigmented lesions.
- Usually solitary, though multiple lesions can occur.

## Risk Factors
- More common in adults, especially women.
- History of minor local trauma (insect bite, shaving nick) at the
  site in some cases, though many arise without a clear trigger.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis is usually clinical, aided by the dimple sign and dermoscopic
features (central white patch/scar-like area with a peripheral pigment
network). Biopsy can be performed if the diagnosis is uncertain or the
lesion has atypical features.

## Treatment
No treatment is medically necessary. Surgical excision can be considered
if the lesion is symptomatic (tender, catches on clothing) or for
cosmetic reasons, though excision can leave a scar comparable in size to
the lesion itself.

## Prognosis & Follow-up
Excellent — dermatofibromas are benign and typically persist unchanged
for years or slowly regress; malignant transformation is essentially
not a concern for typical lesions. Routine monitoring is only needed if
a lesion begins to grow rapidly, change in color asymmetrically, or
otherwise loses its classic features (which should prompt biopsy to
exclude other diagnoses).

## When to Seek Care
Evaluation is reasonable for a new firm nodule to confirm the diagnosis,
or if an existing lesion starts growing, bleeding, or changing shape.
